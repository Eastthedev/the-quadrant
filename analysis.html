// 01

Why your win rate goes up

The original trader targets 10R and wins 60% of the time. That means 60% of trades travel the full distance from entry to 10R. The critical insight: every winning trade passes through 5R on its way to 10R.

So when you cut TP to 5R, you capture all of those 60% winners — plus you additionally capture trades that would have reversed between 5R and 10R, that the original trader counted as losses. Those trades reached 5R but didn't reach 10R. For you, they're wins.

Conservatively estimating that another 10–20% of trades fall into that "reached 5R but not 10R" bucket, your win rate at 5R TP moves to roughly 70–80%.

// key insight

You are not taking a worse version of the same trade. You are taking a higher probability trade at a lower TP, then using The Quadrant to restore and exceed the R:R you gave up by targeting 5R instead of 10R.

// 04

Win rate per quadrant — what changes

Q1 fills immediately at the original entry. Its win rate is your full ~75%. But Q2, Q3, and Q4 only fill if price dips into their zone first — meaning they fill on deeper pullbacks. Two things happen when a quadrant fills deeper:

1. Fewer fills. Not every trade will pull back to Q4. Some go straight from Q1 to TP. Deeper quadrants fill less often — but when they do, the entry is structurally better.

2. Higher win rate when filled. A trade that dips to Q3 before reversing strongly toward TP is exhibiting deeper accumulation. That kind of price behavior — deep sweep, sharp reversal — tends to follow through to target more reliably than a trade that barely dips at all.

// 05

The worst case scenario

All four quadrants fill and all four get stopped out. This is the full loss scenario — price sweeps through the entire SL zone without reversing. Total loss = 4R across four accounts.

At a 75% win rate, this full stop-out happens roughly 25% of trades on Q1 and decreasingly less on deeper quadrants. But even if all four fill and all four stop out on the same trade — one Q1 win returning 20R covers five complete wipeouts with R to spare.

// Worst case: all 4 accounts stopped out
Total loss = −4R

// One Q1 win covers
20R ÷ 4R = 5 full wipeouts

// Required win rate to break even across all 4 accounts
1 ÷ (1 + 20) = 4.8%  // you're at ~75%
// bottom line
You've built a structurally superior system

The original trader needs a 60% win rate to make their 1:10 setup work. You need 4.8% to break even on Q1 alone — and you're sitting at roughly 75%. That gap between your actual win rate and your break-even rate is your margin of safety. It's enormous.

Cutting TP to 5R doesn't weaken the trade. It raises your win rate, and The Quadrant structure converts that 5R target into 1:20 through 1:23 R:R across your four accounts. You've taken someone else's good system and built a mathematically superior version of it on top.

The only variable you don't control is the signal quality. Everything else — the R:R amplification, the win rate improvement, the risk management — is structural and systematic. Vet your signal sources carefully. The system will handle everything else.
